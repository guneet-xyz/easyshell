`sed` can print a **range** of lines, from a line matching one pattern to the next line matching another:

```bash
sed -n '/^START/,/^END/p' file.txt
```

`-n` stops `sed` from printing every line on its own, and `p` prints the lines in the range. Remember that `[` and `]` are special in regular expressions, so escape them: `\[`.
